ASICS Gel-Nimbus vs Nike Vomero: Nimbus 28 or Vomero 18?
Compare the ASICS Gel-Nimbus 28 and Nike Vomero 18 on cushioning, fit, weight, ride, and value to choose your premium neutral trainer.
Quick Hits
- •Choose the Gel-Nimbus 28 for the lighter catalog weight, 8mm drop, and calm maximum-cushion daily and recovery ride.
- •Choose the Vomero 18 for dual-layer ZoomX and ReactX cushioning with a softer, more rolling heel-led character.
- •The Nimbus costs $10 more but has a lower published men's reference weight; both offer standard and wide catalog sizing.

ASICS Gel-Nimbus vs Nike Vomero
Quick Verdict
Choose the ASICS Gel-Nimbus 28 if you want maximum cushioning in the lighter package. Its audited 9.9-ounce men's weight, 8mm drop, and 43.5/35.5mm stack suit easy, recovery, and long mileage without feeling as heavy as the Nike.
Choose the Nike Vomero 18 if you want a softer, more heel-led ride. ZoomX over ReactX creates a distinctly cushioned sensation, while the 10mm drop and 40/30mm stack favor runners who enjoy a substantial rearfoot.

ASICS Gel-Nimbus 28 Overview
The Nimbus 28 uses FF BLAST PLUS with rearfoot PureGEL and a HYBRID ASICSGRIP outsole. It is ASICS' premium neutral comfort trainer, with a broad platform and a rocker that encourages smooth transitions rather than fast turnover.
OutdoorGearLab found substantial comfort but did not treat the shoe as a speed option. Its catalog weight is the practical surprise: version 28 provides maximum geometry while having a lower published reference weight than the men's Vomero figure.
Nike Vomero 18 Overview
The Vomero 18 layers ZoomX over ReactX. Nike positions it as maximum-comfort road footwear, and RTINGS' testing supports an easy and long-run role rather than racing. The 10mm drop and soft heel make its intended landing character clear.
The shoe is heavier, but the extra mass accompanies a dense upper and dual-foam platform. Runners who equate soft compression with comfort may prefer it immediately; those who dislike sink or bulk may not.
Ride and Cushioning
The Nimbus provides a controlled, rolling ride. Its foam and PureGEL reduce harshness, but the platform is not designed around pronounced trampoline rebound. The 8mm drop distributes the transition more evenly than the Nike.
The Vomero feels softer and more rear-loaded. ZoomX supplies initial compression while ReactX stabilizes the base. That construction is comfortable at relaxed pace, though the 10.6-ounce men's weight makes quick changes of pace less appealing.
Ride winner: Nimbus for light, controlled cruising; Vomero for soft heel-first comfort.
Fit and Sizing
Both catalog entries offer standard and wide. The Nimbus generally presents a padded, premium upper and broad platform. The Vomero's fit can feel more tapered through the front; Nike's own customer summary indicates many runners use their normal size, while wider feet may need more room.
Start with your usual running-shoe length and compare actual forefoot volume. A width label does not guarantee the same internal shape across brands. Our choosing running shoes guide explains a practical fit check.
Weight and Pace Range
The Nimbus has lower published weight references, but the sources are not a matched scale comparison and cannot establish a performance advantage. Neither model is a workout specialist. The Nimbus is useful from recovery through steady long running; the Vomero is best when pace remains relaxed.
Runners wanting a livelier ASICS option should compare the ASICS Novablast 6. Nike runners seeking even more cushion can examine the Nike Vomero Plus, though that moves into a different price and stack bracket.
Stability and Ground Feel
Both shoes are neutral. Their wide platforms can feel planted, but neither substitutes for a cataloged stability shoe. The Nimbus' firmer control and lower weight make it feel more composed. The Vomero's softer heel can create more movement for runners who compress one side unevenly.
Ground feel is limited in both, especially compared with a conventional daily trainer. The Nimbus has 43.5/35.5mm of stack; the Vomero has 40/30mm. Runners who want road feedback should choose outside this matchup.
Value
The Vomero costs MSRP: $10 less, but the Nimbus counters with substantially lower catalog weight and a maximum cushion classification. At these prices, the better value is the shoe that covers more weekly mileage comfortably, not the one with the lower sticker alone.
The Nimbus is compelling for a three-role easy, recovery, and long-run shoe. The Vomero is compelling for runners who specifically want its soft dual-foam feel. Our best cushioned running shoes guide includes both design philosophies.
Choose the Gel-Nimbus 28 If...
- You want maximum cushioning at the lower weight.
- An 8mm drop and controlled rocker suit your stride.
- Recovery and long-run use must share one shoe.
- You prefer a broad, planted neutral platform.
Choose the Vomero 18 If...
- You enjoy soft ZoomX compression over a stable ReactX base.
- A 10mm drop and heel-focused ride feel natural.
- You prioritize comfort over weight and pace changes.
- Saving MSRP: $10 matters more than the scale difference.
Final Verdict
The Gel-Nimbus 28 is the stronger all-around cushioned trainer because it combines maximum catalog stack, a lower published reference weight, and a controlled ride. The Vomero 18 wins for runners who want a softer, more traditional heel-led sensation. Neither is objectively more comfortable; the decisive question is whether calm lightness or compressive softness feels better underfoot. For broader long-run choices, see our best long-run shoes.
Key Takeaway
The Gel-Nimbus 28 is the lighter, calmer maximum-cushion option, while the Vomero 18 is softer and more heel-oriented; fit and preferred foam character should decide the matchup.
