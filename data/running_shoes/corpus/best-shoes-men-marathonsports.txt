Factors to Consider When Shopping for Men’s Running Shoes
The perfect pair of running shoes for one person may not be quite right for somebody else. That’s because finding the right shoe relies on a variety of factors.
Running Style
Running shoes are built differently for varying styles of running. Men’s running shoes for the track are different from those made for running long distances or trail running. It’s essential to consider the style of running you prefer and choose a shoe that suits it best.
Long-distance runners will likely find more comfort in shoes with a lot of cushioning that are easier on the joints. However, those focused on speed will likely want a lighter-weight, more responsive running shoe with a lower profile for form corrections and quicker steps.
Some runners prefer purchasing one comfortable and reliable running shoe that can perform at the gym and in other workout settings too. Many of the shoes listed below are suitable for long-distance runs, gym days, pickup football games, and various other physical activities in between.
Former Injuries
Runners with knee problems or other former injuries may require extra support and shock absorption. More cushioning reduces the impact that travels from the foot up the leg and to the knee. That means less aching in joints and feet. On the opposite side, if you have a history of Achilles pain, you may opt for a higher heel-to-toe offset, helping lighten the load on your calf muscles but adding a little more to your knees.
Foot Shape
Some runners may need wider shoes to ensure comfort and breathability for their wider feet. Many of the best men’s running shoes are available in varying widths to accommodate different runners. Our reviewers appreciate running shoes with wider toe boxes for superior breathability and fewer blisters or broken toenails.
Individuals with higher arches or flat feet may look for running shoes with more stability and other features to support their needs and help avoid injury. There are more factors to consider beyond foot shape; even runners with “normal” arches may look for shoe features like heel counters or medial posts. What’s important is finding a shoe that works best for your foot shape, gait cycle, and range of motion.
More Than Appearance
Aesthetics are the first features most shoppers notice when looking for the ideal pair of running shoes. However, we suggest you put appearance toward the end of your list of criteria.
Many runners make mistakes by choosing shoes because of how they look. Yes, our reviewers do appreciate a nice-looking running shoe, but they never recommend choosing a shoe that looks cool but doesn’t fit quite right.
It’s most important that you consider your need for stability, cushioning, and any other fit requirements you have before assessing a running shoe’s look. Fortunately, there are a lot of high-quality running shoes available, and many of them are great-looking.
